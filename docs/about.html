A GUIDE TO ISOMASS

Change the form.
Keep the whole.

Isomass is an interactive mathematical playground about conservation, structure, and connection. You divide one whole into pieces, choose which family to emphasize, and watch their combined pattern respond.

The pieces always add up to 64 abstract units. The arrangement can change dramatically while that total stays fixed. That is the central idea you can explore with your own hands.

01 / CONSERVATION

Many pieces. Still 64.

Start with a single region of capacity 64. Divide replaces the selected region with two equal halves: 64 becomes 32 + 32; a 32 becomes 16 + 16. The smallest possible piece has capacity 1.

Rejoin family collapses the selected region's parent back into one region. It includes every piece inside that parent, even if some pieces were divided again. The preview beneath the edit buttons tells you how many regions will be combined.

The outer ring maps each piece to an arc proportional to its capacity. A piece of 16 takes one quarter of the ring. The selected piece is gold. Dividing or rejoining changes the partition; selecting a piece changes the emphasis.

What stays fixed? The sum of all region capacities is always 64. The number of regions, their sizes, their connections, and the wave's height can change.

02 / CONNECTION

Your selection finds a family.

A connected family is a continuous run of adjacent regions with the same size. Selecting one member highlights its connected peers in teal. Every member of this active family receives twice the weight in the combined wave; other regions receive their ordinary weight.

Try Contrast. It has two 16-unit pieces and four 8-unit pieces. Select a 16, then an 8. The structure is unchanged, but a different family now gets extra weight, so the wave changes. Select another 8 in the same family and the wave stays exactly the same.

Equal size alone is not enough: a differently sized piece can separate two families. The underlying partition runs from 0 to 64 in a line, even though it is displayed as a circle. The first and last pieces are not automatically neighbors across the circular seam.

What do the small gold dots mean?

They mark equal-size regions whose positions reflect across the middle of the partition. Reflection is a geometric relationship, separate from belonging to the active connected family.

03 / PATTERN

One combined wave, drawn two ways.

Each region contributes a repeating triangular wave. Its size determines the scale, its position shifts the phase, and active-family membership increases its weight. The model adds these contributions at 256 sample positions.

The outer ring

Your partition: how many pieces you have, how large they are, and which family is active.

The inner portrait

The 256 combined samples wrapped around a circle. It makes symmetries and repeating structures visible.

The lower trace

The same samples laid out along a line. It makes peaks and differences easier to compare.

This is a deterministic drawing rule: the same partition and active family give the same samples. The conserved quantity is the capacity of the pieces. The wave's height and area are not conserved quantities.

Isomass is a mathematical exploration tool. Its abstract units and waves do not represent a calibrated physical material, energy measurement, or prediction of a real-world system.

04 / EXPLORE

Turn a change into a discovery.

  1. Compare two forms. Choose Compare forms to hold reference A beside editable B. Both use the same visual scale. The comparison shows how many of the 256 wave samples changed and compares region sizes, selection, and connections.
  2. Ask a small question. Does changing only the selected region change the wave? What happens if you divide a single member of an equal-size family? Can you return to the original pattern?
  3. Undo and redo. Explore an edit and return to your previous form. During scored challenges, these count as moves when they change the partition.
  4. Save a named form. Keep up to 24 discoveries in this browser. Share a link to preserve the partition and selected region elsewhere; comparison links also preserve A.
  5. Export an image. Download a PNG of the current form or comparison to show what you found.
  6. Take the guided challenge. Complete all three investigations, record points, choose a public name, and submit your best scores when the leaderboard is connected.

On a phone, use the region picker and arrow buttons for small arcs. On a keyboard, use Space to divide, Backspace to rejoin, Up/Down to select, and Ctrl/Command Z to undo. The buttons offer the same actions.

05 / UNDER THE DRAWING

The model, in detail.

How the partition is represented

The whole is the root of a binary tree. A leaf is an undivided region; a branch holds two equal halves. At depth d, a leaf has width 64 / 2^d. Maximum depth is 6, so all leaves have integer capacity between 1 and 64. The browser checks that valid partitions still sum to 64.

Links encode this tree with L for a leaf and B followed by its left and right children for a branch. L is the whole; BLL is two halves. Selection uses a tree identifier; the picker presents ordinary region numbers for people.

The exact wave calculation

For sample position x from 0 to 255, each region has starting position s, width w, and membership a (1 if active, 0 otherwise):

phase = floor((x + 3 × s) × 64 / w) mod 256
pulse = min(phase, 256 − phase)
contribution = floor((1 + a) × w × pulse / 2)
combined sample = sum of all region contributions

The portrait and trace render this same sample array. Two equal-width regions reflect one another when their starts s and t satisfy s + t + w = 64.

Browser edition and the original experiment

This GitHub Pages edition runs its partition and drawing calculations in browser JavaScript. The original desktop experiment explored a conservation proof in Idris and separate Flix/Bend runtimes. Those native components are not executing inside this web page; the browser edition uses runtime checks and automated tests.

The leaderboard is a separate service: it checks each submitted action sequence against the same challenge rules and stores each player's best scores. You can explore the playground without signing in.
